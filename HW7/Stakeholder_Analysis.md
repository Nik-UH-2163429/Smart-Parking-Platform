
# HW7 — Stakeholder Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

The Smart Parking Platform is a software-based system designed to help drivers locate available parking spaces, view parking availability, and improve parking management efficiency.

This stakeholder analysis identifies individuals and groups who influence the project or are affected by its development and implementation. Stakeholders are evaluated using a Power–Interest Grid to determine appropriate communication and engagement strategies.

## 2. Purpose of Stakeholder Analysis

The purpose of this analysis is to:
- Identify the project's key stakeholders.
- Evaluate each stakeholder's level of power and interest.
- Establish appropriate communication and engagement strategies.
- Reduce misunderstandings and improve stakeholder participation.
- Support effective project decision-making.

## 4. Power–Interest Grid

The Power–Interest Grid classifies stakeholders based on their influence over project decisions and their level of concern regarding project outcomes.

| | Low Interest | High Interest |
|---|---|---|
| **High Power** | **Keep Satisfied** — Project Sponsor / Investor, University Instructor | **Manage Closely** — Project Manager, Parking Facility Management |
| **Low Power** | **Monitor** — General Public | **Keep Informed** — End Users / Drivers, Software Developer |

The Software Developer is classified in the Keep Informed quadrant for engagement planning, although the role has medium operational influence rather than strictly low power.

### Quadrant 1 — Manage Closely

**Stakeholders:** Project Manager and Parking Facility Management.

These stakeholders have significant influence over project requirements, decisions, and implementation. They require frequent communication and direct involvement in major project decisions.

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Conduct regular progress meetings.
- Review project milestones and deliverables.
- Address concerns and risks promptly.
- Include stakeholders in significant decisions.

### Quadrant 2 — Keep Satisfied

**Stakeholders:** Project Sponsor / Investor and University Instructor.

These stakeholders have substantial authority over project funding, approval, or evaluation but may not participate in daily development activities.

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Provide concise milestone updates.
- Communicate major project risks and schedule changes.
- Request approval when necessary.
- Ensure deliverables satisfy established expectations.

### Quadrant 3 — Keep Informed

**Stakeholders:** End Users / Drivers and Software Developer.

These stakeholders have a strong interest in project functionality and outcomes. Their feedback is valuable for improving usability and technical performance.

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Collect feedback during development and testing.
- Share feature updates and development progress.
- Communicate changes that affect system functionality.
- Encourage participation in usability testing and technical reviews.

### Quadrant 4 — Monitor

**Stakeholders:** General Public.

These stakeholders have limited direct involvement in project decisions and may only be indirectly affected by the system.

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Provide general project information when appropriate.
- Monitor public concerns or feedback.
- Increase communication if stakeholder interest changes.

## 6. Conclusion

Stakeholder management is essential to the successful development of the Smart Parking Platform. Using the Power–Interest Grid allows the project team to prioritize communication based on stakeholder influence and interest.

By managing high-power stakeholders closely, keeping interested stakeholders informed, and monitoring other groups appropriately, the project can maintain alignment with its objectives and reduce potential conflicts throughout development.
